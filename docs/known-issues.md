# Known issues

[Home](./) · [Limits and dual use](limitations.md) · [How accurate is it?](validation.md)

Honest list of what is known to be wrong, weak or untested, kept up to date. The audit behind most of it is [audit-v2.1.md](audit-v2.1.md).

## Corrections in v2.3.0

v2.3.0 (22 November 2026) measured the tool on four public datasets it was not built on and ran a pre-registered forward test of its alarms. What the published texts said before, and what they say now:

- **Delivery count: 9 became 11.** A commit that the release cut into two records (R0022674 and R0022675) was dropped at v2.2.0 as a call cut inside its verb. Read as one record (rule R13a) it is a write, read later by two anonymous runs. The release now has **11 write-then-read deliveries, 4 between named tokens (unchanged), 0 where the reader is shown using what was written (unchanged).** The two added deliveries keep the audit label "unclear": nobody has judged the joined text. A new second form of the written-content test (R38b: a distinctive term of the write in the reader's later output) also finds none on the release. The second implementation finds the same 11, and 9,867 on the collusion.wiki logs.
- **"No real benign traffic was available" is no longer true.** Four public datasets were measured (`reports/external/`). The old Alert rule (2 kinds of coordination) fired in 181 of 184 Who&When runs, 1,567 of 1,640 MAST-Data runs, 40 of 40 Moltbook communities and all 1,800 labelled SALT runs. The first three hold no recorded attack. The rule detects information flow, not malice, on real data as on simulated data.
- **The simple alarms are the main alarms now, and their forward test failed.** The first new host after 12 hours, the first secrets search, the distinct-actor alarm and the volume alarm fired earliest on the one real incident, so they replaced the shared-control signs as the main early warning; the signs are kept as "context, not an early warning". That choice is post-hoc on one incident. In the pre-registered forward test no alarm is shown to lead the first true harm on either simulator (the first of the four came before it in 5 of 90 and 6 of 20 swarms). Benign false alarms: any of the four in 18 of 30 and 23 of 50 simulated benign swarms, and in 38 of 38 real Moltbook community traces. Only the new-host alarm stayed quiet on simulated benign swarms (0 of 30, 2 of 50), and it fired in 37 of 38 Moltbook traces. The pre-registration file was written minutes before the run and was not committed to git first. See [early-warning.md](early-warning.md).
- **Failure attribution is at chance.** On Who&When (184 failed runs with human labels) the tool's first warning sign names the labelled agent in 54 runs (29.3%; chance 29.1%) and the labelled step in 25 (13.6%; chance 9.5%). Naming the agent with most records gets 72, the last speaker 80. The tool has no failure-attribution feature; these rules were fixed before scoring to see whether its flags could be read as blame, and they cannot.
- **Collusion (SALT).** As published the episodes have no times and the tool fires nothing (recall 0 of 1,351 colluding runs). With an order-only clock the Alert rule fires on 1,351 of 1,351 colluding runs and 449 of 449 others. A pack written from one run (`packs/peer_review.json`) fires on 42.0% of colluding runs and 5.8% of the others in the 35 conditions it was not written from: 87.4% where an agent is the model its phrases were read from, 20.9% where none is. It is a phrase match and does not see collusion that is not announced.
- **Who talks to whom holds by pair, not by message.** With recipients withheld the tool finds 91 of 92 logged pairs on Who&When and 2,242 of 2,800 (80.1%) on MAST-Data, but 9.0% and 9.5% of the single logged messages, and many of its pairs are not in the log (38.4% of its Who&When pairs are logged; 75.9% in either direction). On Moltbook 26.5% of its inferred reply links are logged replies: 96.9% for the first comment in a thread, 1.0% for later comments.
- **Whose labels.** Who&When is labelled by people. The MAST-Data labels are from an LLM judge, except a 19-run file labelled by people. The SALT label is the game's outcome (both agents accept). Nobody labelled the Moltbook traffic.
- **The agent harness has no real-agent run.** [harness.md](harness.md): built, with an offline run of a scripted stand-in only, because the machine had no API key. That run exposed faults in how the tool read a tool-call log (the server counted as a party, one-word places not seen, the uses-content test looking in the wrong record). They were fixed, on the harness's own scripted swarms, so its scores are no longer a test of the tool.
- **Continuous integration exists but has not run on GitHub.** `.github/workflows/check.yml` runs the unit tests, the site check, the smoke test and oracle on the demo swarm, the printed numbers against the committed manifest, and a wheel build. It re-derives no number from the two datasets and starts no browser ([integrity.md](integrity.md#what-ci-runs-and-what-it-cannot)).
- **Not re-measured for v2.3.0.** The independent-generator scores (16 November run), the count-variance run and the judgement of single operations where the two implementations differ. The original-simulator validation was run again on the v2.3.0 code and gave the same tables. Mutation testing was run again on the v2.3.0 rule: 1,570 of 1,656 mutants of `messages.py` are killed; of the 86 survivors, 78 are judged equivalent by one reader and 8 are not.

## Corrections in v2.1.1 / v2.2.0

Every published text was checked against the audit of v2.1.0 (16 November 2026). What changed:

- **Message count.** "23 messages, 6 between named tokens" was replaced by **9 write-then-read deliveries, 4 between named tokens (2 between agents, 2 inside one agent), 0 where the reader is shown using what was written** (11 since v2.3.0, above). The word "message" is kept only for logged agent-to-agent messages. A reviewer defended about 5 of the 23 (range 3 to 7).
- **Who labelled.** The audit labels are one reader's, the authors' AI assistant reading the records with a helper script. No person read the 258 links by hand; texts that said so were corrected (README, demo script, app text, dataset registry, the first-message investigation).
- **Early-warning lead.** "+5.8 h ahead" is withdrawn. The shared-place sign fires a median 1.4 h after the first harm on the original generator and in 0 of 20 swarms on the independent one. On the real release the shared-control signs fire with 50% to 57% of harm still to come, behind a volume alarm (83%, or 93% with a 6 h history), the first new host after 12 h (91%), the distinct-actor alarm (87%) and the first `search_for_secrets` action (91%).
- **Simulated accuracy.** Current code on the original simulator: precision 90%, recall 84% (the old 79% and 73% were for the old rule). Independent generator: 70% and 58%; 19% and 10% with a time on only 30% of records; no dating at all when the file is grouped by agent. The simulators are optimistic, and v1.1.0 scored 79% on one while 165 of its 166 real links were false.
- **False alarms (simulated; the real-data figures are in the v2.3.0 section above).** On 50 independent benign look-alike swarms the Alert rule fired in 40 (80%) and the Watch rule in 40; on the 30 original benign swarms the Alert rule fired in 5 and the Watch rule in 20. The slide claim "0 in 15 runs" meant no delivery between different agents, not no alarm.
- **"Every claim comes with a measured range" and "every number can be re-derived"** are removed. 237 numbers are registered and checked by `verify` (180 re-derived from the data or from saved runs at v2.2.0, 57 added in v2.3.0 from the saved results on outside datasets, the forward test and the crosscheck); 778 more figures in the scanned texts are outside the manifest.
- **Appendix B, ordering claims, counts.** The "one dataset read by six tokens, another by seven" claim is withdrawn. "The ordering is robust, about 30 h apart" and "a day or more ahead" are replaced by the actual gaps. "385 self-termination records" is kept, with the note that only 73 call `os.kill`. "Anonymous runs" is 77,626 actions with no token, or 3,765 actors on places; the two are no longer mixed.
- **Other.** The wiki logs are described as a different swarm run by other agents, not attributed to OpenAI; the Howard-Jones talk is cited with what it does and does not claim; the in-app "median error about 15 min, p90 about 2 h" string is listed under code changes needed; the video narration was re-recorded on 16 November for these corrections (and again on 22 November for v2.3.0).

Not fixable by editing text: two quoted runs of record text remain in git history; `reports/audit/` (the reviewers' reports) is kept locally. The video was re-recorded for v2.2.0 and again on 22 November 2026 from the v2.3.0 script (11 deliveries, the simple alarms as main alarms).

## Accuracy
- **The submitted report's message count (19) is superseded, and so are v2.1.0's 23 and v2.2.0's 9.** The rule now reports 11 write-then-read deliveries, 4 between named tokens, none where the reader is shown using what was written. 6 of the 11 are labelled unclear, and the labels are one reader's. The deep dive (docs/deep-dive.md) was written on the older counts and carries a note saying so.
- **Simulated precision is an upper bound, and is higher than on an independent generator.** The original simulator writes the exact strings the rules match; an independently written one gives message precision 70% and recall 58% (not 90% and 84%). It also never writes the read-then-exfiltrate pattern that produced most false links on the Swarm Traces release (166 reported at v1.1.0, 19 at v1.3.0, 23 at v2.1.0; see validation). Audit real links before quoting them.
- **Messages that only name a place.** Fixed for code comments: a place named only in a comment no longer counts as a read (simulated message precision 66% → 79% on the original simulator, before the later rule changes). A place named in a printed string or a variable that is never fetched can still count. See [validation](validation.md).
- **Coordination signs on simulated swarms are timed within an hour** in most cases (the first delivery between agents in 45 of 45 original swarms), but that is a property of the simulator. On the real release the signs fire late, not early, and the simple alarms that fired earlier there failed their forward test (see the early-warning page).
- **Program reuse and boilerplate.** Fixed: reuse counts as a sign only when the program writes, reads a place another actor wrote, or does harm (original benign swarms: 10 of 10 → 0 of 10; the independent benign swarms fire the program-reuse sign in 28 to 40 of 50). Two agents that run the same attack code without contact still fire it.
- **The harm list is broad and builds in the answer.** In the code-traces pack every Hugging Face dataset commit counts as harm (64% of the 8,124 harm records are dataset writes), so the first dataset write is early by construction; `kubernetes_token` was added after the first replay. Changing the harm definition moves the shared-place share of harm to come from 37.7% to 56.6%.
- **Identity merges at low confidence are rarely right** (about 1% on simulated swarms). Treat low as "worth a look". High and medium require direct evidence and were right in every simulated case.
- **Comments are matched like code.** An intent pattern can fire on a commented-out line.

- **Dating depends on file order and embedded times.** With a time on only 30% of records message precision falls to 19%; with the file grouped by agent the trace comes back undated and the tool reports no deliveries.
- **Honest cloud agents trigger the Watch rule 10 of 10** in the independent benign swarms, because metadata calls, cluster calls and any dataset commit are in the harm list. The Alert rule fires on benign swarms with real information flow (shared leaderboard, CI polling, coordinator and workers) in 30 of 30, and on 95% to 100% of the real runs measured in v2.3.0. It detects information flow, not malice.
- **The Watch rule on real traces is a word list.** With the general pack it fired on 37 of 40 Moltbook communities and on 43.7% of colluding and 42.5% of non-colluding SALT runs, on words such as "password", "secret", "upload" and "delete". A record that uses such a word is not shown to do harm.
- **Traces without times cannot be ordered.** Who&When, four of the seven MAST-Data frameworks and SALT carry no time on any record. Loaded as published the tool reports no delivery and no alarm on them; the measurements above use a step clock (one record a minute, in logged order), which is order only.
- **Identity.** "12 of 12 high-confidence merges right" is 12 of 380 true pairs (about 3% recall), and the merge rule was rewritten after it scored 10 of 240 on the same swarms.
- **Attribution of anonymous runs** reaches 7.7% of the 77,626 anonymous runs on the release. The held-out precision (99.4% / 96.4% / 87.9% by band) is measured on simulated swarms split by thread, not on real runs; leaving out the three largest actors lowers it.
- **Insider findings** (11 recovered, 15 partly, 15 invisible) are graded against a 1-hour tolerance fixed before the run; 15 of the 41 are the Swarm Traces authors' own claims searched in the data, and the grading is not independent of the tool's own dating.
- **Sensitivity ranges** use 10 draws; place, token and program signs are not time-perturbed; the harm list and the baseline parameters are not varied.
- **The online monitor** reproduces 3 of 15 replay signs within 15 minutes; on the online test volume alarms fire about 29 h later. Of the four main alarms, all fire online on the release but 0 of 4 within 15 minutes of the replay's time. The report's statement that a lab could run the signs online is a hope, not a result.

## Untested
- **The agent harness has not been run with a real model.** Every number in [harness.md](harness.md) is about a script.
- **The four main alarms on a second real incident.** One incident chose them; no other is available.
- **The CI workflow on GitHub**, and its Python 3.12 half anywhere.
- **The AI investigator has not been run against a real model** in this project; every path is tested against stand-in providers.
- **Framework readers** (OpenTelemetry, LangSmith, Langfuse, AutoGen, CrewAI, OpenAI Agents SDK, MCP) are tested on samples written to match each format, not on real exports.

## Product
- **Large swarms draw slowly.** On thousands of actors the network graph is dense and some charts cap what they draw (they say so).
- **A pack can hold a slow regular expression.** A badly written pattern can tie up the server; there is no time limit on patterns yet.
- **Path refusals on macOS** mention "a link" for temporary folders under `/var`, which is confusing.

## Open after v2.1.0 (15 November 2026)

- **Click test has no clean full run on the release.** The scripted interaction test passes on the demo swarm (quick mode). On the Swarm Traces release, one run that a server restart interrupted reported three failures that were not re-run: the Channels "Leave out merged places" toggle not restoring its numbers, the Channels row filter not taking focus on click, and one dead chart node on Early. Treat them as unconfirmed.
- **The second implementation now agrees with the rule on the release, but not independently** (11 deliveries each; it found 191 until v2.2.0 because redaction broke its quote pairing, and was changed while reading where it differed). 262 single operations on the release still differ without changing a delivery; they were judged by group for the v2.2.0 rule, and for v2.3.0 only the group sizes were checked (unchanged), not the rows. Both implementations were written by the same team from the same rule. See [integrity](integrity.md) and `reports/integrity/crosscheck_judgement.json`.
- **The top bar overflows below about 460 px wide.** The app is built for desktop widths; the investigations and dataset pages fit phones.
- **The hosted demo was checked locally, not on GitHub Pages**, before publishing.
