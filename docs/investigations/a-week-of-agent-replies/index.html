A week of agent replies: who answers whom, and how often the alarms ring

On Moltbook the accounts are AI agents, and every reply records what it answers. In one week of 40 communities the tool's own guess at who answered whom is a logged reply for 26% of its links, and its Alert rule rings in all 40.

10 claims · 0 records cited · 416 words. Select a marked claim to see the evidence behind it.

Real traffic, with the replies written down

Moltbook is a social network whose accounts are AI agents. Researchers at SimulaMet archive it by passive polling and publish the archive under an open licence. Evidence 1: We took one week of it: 187,738 posts and comments in the 40 busiest communities1. No incident is recorded for that week. It is the ordinary talk of agents, with spam and promotion in it.

Every comment records what it answers: another comment, or the post. That is a known answer to the question this tool was built for. Evidence 2: The week holds 41,737 replies from one agent to another2.

Reading the log

Our reader hands those fields to the tool, and Evidence 3: the tool lists all 41,737 of them as logged direct messages3. That checks the reader. It is not an accuracy.

Inferring it

The Swarm Traces release has no reply field. There the tool infers: a record in a shared place has read the latest earlier record there by someone else. We let it apply the same rule inside each thread and compared its links with the logged replies. Evidence 4: 26% of its links are logged replies, and it finds 26% of them4. Evidence 5: Counted by pair of agents the figures are 30% and 31%5.

The misses have one cause. Evidence 6: When only one other agent has written in a thread, the rule is right 97% of the time6: there is nothing else to answer. Evidence 7: For later comments it is right 1% of the time7. Most comments answer the post, and the rule ties each one to the comment just before it. A write followed by a read shows that the text was there to be read. It does not show whom the reader was answering.

The alarms

Each community's week is one trace. Evidence 8: The Alert rule fired in 40 of 408, a median of 2.2 hours in. It asks for two kinds of coordination between named agents, and on a network made for replies those appear at once. Evidence 9: The Watch rule fired in 379; its commonest match was the plain word secret. Evidence 10: The volume baseline fired in 3910. On traffic like this an alarm says that agents are talking, and nothing more.

One week of one platform, chosen before any result. The archive's authors say that comment coverage is partial, so the replies here are the ones that were collected. No agent is named and no post is quoted on this page; the table that holds the handles of the agents' owners was not downloaded.
